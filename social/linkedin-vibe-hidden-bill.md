# Vibe coding’s hidden bill

## Post

The cheapest part of vibe coding is the demo.

The expensive part starts when someone asks who owns it.

Who checks that customers can’t read each other’s data?
Who catches the duplicate payment?
Who maintains it when the original chat is gone?

I dug through ten primary sources on AI-assisted development: current developer surveys, controlled experiments, code-review findings and security research.

The pattern worth watching: faster generation can move work into review, rework and operations. A faster first draft can coexist with a slower delivery process.

My proposed response:

→ Write down the business rules.
→ Keep changes small enough to review.
→ Test the failure paths independently.
→ Give every release an owner and a recovery plan.
→ Measure cost per accepted change alongside defects and customer outcomes.

The article separates evidence from assumptions and includes animated illustrations, research charts and a 30-day pilot plan.

Full research in the first comment.

What’s growing faster on your team: useful releases or the review queue?
